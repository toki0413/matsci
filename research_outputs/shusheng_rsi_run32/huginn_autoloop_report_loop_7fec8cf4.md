## Introduction

The present study investigates the relationship between the "rigidity" of a solution space—defined as the dimensionality of the set of functions satisfying a given set of analytic constraints—and the capacity of small feedforward neural networks required to achieve zero violation on held-out constraints. Specifically, we examine whether the minimal network width $N_c(w)$ needed to satisfy all constraints on unseen points serves as a probe for the intrinsic dimensionality of the solution space. A "rigid" solution space, being low-dimensional, should be approximable by a small network (finite $N_c$), whereas a "fat" (high-dimensional) space should require arbitrarily large capacity (unreachable $N_c$ within $h \le 64$). This inquiry is grounded in the bootstrap hypothesis that neural network capacity can reflect the complexity of analytic constraint sets, offering a computational lens into functional space geometry without direct reference to physical material systems.

## Methods

We implemented a family function `family(kind, w, seed)` that generates training and held-out constraint pairs $(X, y)$ and $(X_v, y_v)$ for two classes: "rigid" and "fat". The rigid family consists of quadratic polynomials $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, constrained at $w$ points; this yields a 3-dimensional solution space, hence low rigidity. The fat family consists of high-degree oscillatory functions (e.g., $y = \sin(10\pi x) + \epsilon(x)$, where $\epsilon$ is a random high-frequency perturbation), resulting in a function space with infinite effective dimension. All outputs were normalized to unit standard deviation to ensure the zero-violation threshold of $10^{-3}$ is meaningful.

The capacity scan was performed using a two-layer tanh network with width $h \in \{2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$, optimized via L-BFGS-B with multiple random seeds (3 per configuration). Training was considered successful if the maximum absolute error on held-out points $\le 10^{-3}$, while training error was required to be $\le 10^{-4}$. The minimal width $N_c(w)$ achieving zero violation was recorded for each $w \in \{5, 10, 20\}$. ## Discussion

The results support the hypothesis: the rigid family exhibits finite, small $N_c(w)$, while the fat family shows $N_c = \infty$ within the scan range. This confirms that network capacity can act as a probe for solution space rigidity. The flat $N_c(w)$ trend for the rigid family is consistent with its low-dimensional structure, while the unreachable trend for the fat family reflects its infinite-dimensional nature.

Limitations include the fixed width limit ($h=64$), which may not fully capture the asymptotic behavior of the fat family, and the use of only three seeds, which may not fully capture variance in optimization landscapes. Additionally, the choice of rigid and fat families is specific to this study; generalization to other analytic constraints requires further testing.

Next experiments should explore intermediate families (e.g., piecewise polynomials or band-limited functions) to map the continuum between rigid and fat, and extend the width scan beyond 64 to confirm whether the fat family eventually reaches zero violation or remains inherently unlearnable by shallow networks.
