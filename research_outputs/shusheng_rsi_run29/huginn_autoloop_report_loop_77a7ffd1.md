## Introduction

We investigate whether the generalization behavior of small feedforward networks can serve as a probe for solution space rigidity in constraint satisfaction problems. Specifically, we examine how the minimum network capacity (hidden layer width) required to achieve zero violation on held-out constraints scales with the number of training constraints $w$. 

In rigid solution spaces (low-dimensional/unique), a small network should satisfy all constraints regardless of $w$, yielding non-increasing $N_c(w)$. In fat solution spaces (high-dimensional/continuous families), more constraints may require larger networks to resolve ambiguities, yielding increasing $N_c(w)$. This approach treats network capacity as a diagnostic tool for the intrinsic dimensionality of the solution manifold defined by analytical constraints.

## Methods
