## Introduction

The question of whether the function space of neural networks is rigid or “fat” under a set of analytical constraints is central to understanding generalization in deep learning. In this context, we define **solution space rigidity** as the dimensionality of the set of functions that satisfy a given set of constraints: low dimensionality or uniqueness implies rigidity, while high dimensionality or the existence of continuous families of solutions implies a fat space. We investigate whether the **generalization behavior of neural networks**—specifically, the minimal network capacity required to satisfy all constraints without violation—can serve as a probe for this rigidity.

We focus on a simple but well-defined task: approximating a noisy sine function using small feedforward networks. The number of training constraints (data points) $ w $ is varied, and we measure $ N_c(w) $, the minimal hidden layer width required to achieve zero training violation (loss < $ 10^{-4} $). If $ N_c(w) $ grows sublinearly with $ w $, it suggests the solution space is rigid and efficiently compressible. If it grows superlinearly or linearly, it indicates a fat solution space.
