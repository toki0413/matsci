## Introduction

The central scientific question addressed in this study is whether the "generalization behavior" of small feedforward neural networks can serve as a probe for the rigidity of the solution space defined by analytical constraints. In computational materials science and mathematical modeling, the dimensionality of the solution space—termed "rigidity" (low-dimensional/unique) versus "fatness" (high-dimensional/family)—often dictates the predictability and stability of a system. We propose a bootstrap method: measuring the minimum network width $N_c(w)$ required to achieve zero violation (maximum absolute error $\leq 10^{-3}$) on held-out constraint points as the number of constraints $w$ increases. If $N_c(w)$ remains constant or decreases, the solution space is rigid; if it increases, the space is fat. This study aims to validate this probe using small feedforward networks without mapping the problem to physical material systems, adhering strictly to the specified zero-violation criteria and optimization constraints.
