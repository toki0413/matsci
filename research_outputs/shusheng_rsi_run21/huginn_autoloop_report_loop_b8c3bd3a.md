## Introduction

The question addressed here is whether the generalization behavior of small feedforward networks can serve as a probe for the rigidity of solution spaces in mathematical constraint systems. In computational materials science, such rigidity often determines the uniqueness or multiplicity of stable configurations under given constraints. However, in purely mathematical or machine learning contexts, understanding how network capacity scales with the number of constraints provides insight into the intrinsic dimensionality of the solution space. If a small network can satisfy all constraints (i.e., achieve “zero violation”) even as constraints increase, the solution space is likely low-dimensional or rigid. Conversely, if increasing capacity is required to maintain zero violation as constraints grow, the solution space is likely high-dimensional or “fat.” This study investigates this relationship using small two-layer neural networks as function approximators under controlled analytic constraints.

Limitations include the use of a fixed network architecture (two-layer tanh), limited width range, and synthetic constraint families that may not represent all possible analytic systems. The optimization, while robust via L-BFGS-B, may still converge to local minima, though multi-start mitigation reduces this risk. Next steps should involve testing with higher-dimensional input spaces, deeper networks, and more complex constraint families (e.g., nonlinear or stochastic constraints) to assess the generality of the observed rigidity. Furthermore, direct measurement of solution space dimensionality via tangent space analysis or manifold learning on trained weights could provide complementary validation.
