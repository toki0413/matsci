## Introduction

This study investigates whether the generalization behavior of small two-layer feedforward neural networks can serve as a probe for the rigidity of a function space defined by analytic constraints. In computational materials science and inverse design, solution spaces are often characterized by their dimensionality: a *rigid* space (low-dimensional, unique) versus a *fat* space (high-dimensional, continuous family). We define the *capacity threshold* $N_c(w)$ as the minimum network width required to achieve zero violation (held-out error $\le 10^{-3}$) on $w$ constraints. If $N_c(w)$ remains small and flat as $w$ increases, the space is rigid; if $N_c(w)$ is unreachable (error remains $>10^{-3}$ even at width $h=64$), the space is fat. This experiment tests these definitions using a rigid family (linear functions) and a fat family (high-degree polynomials), ensuring numerical stability through output normalization.

## Methods

Conversely, the fat family (high-degree polynomial constraints) was unreachable even at maximum width $h=64$, indicating a high-dimensional space where $w$ constraints are insufficient to constrain the function within the $10^{-3}$ tolerance.

While the space is theoretically rigid (2D), the optimization landscape at very low constraint counts may allow for wider networks to fit noise or alternative local minima before settling on the linear solution.
