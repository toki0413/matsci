## Introduction

We investigate whether the minimal network capacity required to achieve zero violation on held-out constraints—denoted $N_c(w)$—can serve as a probe for the rigidity of a solution space defined by analytic constraints. This approach avoids physical analogies and focuses purely on the behavior of small two-layer feedforward networks under analytic constraint satisfaction.

Training error must be $\leq 10^{-4}$, and held-out error is independently evaluated. The rigid family corresponds to a low-dimensional analytic solution (e.g., polynomial identity), while the fat family introduces high-frequency or underdetermined components to ensure intrinsic ambiguity beyond $w$ constraints.
