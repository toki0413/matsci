## Introduction

We investigate whether the generalization behavior of small feedforward neural networks can serve as a probe for the rigidity of solution spaces defined by analytical constraints. In computational materials science, such rigidity determines whether a system admits a unique solution (rigid) or a continuous family of solutions (fat), with profound implications for predictability and design. Here, we define solution space rigidity by the dimension of the function space satisfying all constraints: low-dimensional or unique solutions are rigid; high-dimensional or continuous families are fat.

Our central question: Can the minimal network width $N_c(w)$ required to achieve zero violation (maximum absolute error $\le 10^{-3}$) on held-out constraints act as a diagnostic for rigidity? We hypothesize that rigid systems exhibit $N_c(w)$ plateauing at a small finite value as the number of constraints $w$ increases, while fat systems require $N_c(w)$ to grow with $w$.

## Methods

We designed a controlled numerical experiment using small fully connected feedforward networks with ReLU activations. The solution space is generated via polynomial basis functions in one dimension, allowing precise control over the number of constraints and underlying functional form.

- **Rigid family:** A 3-coefficient quadratic polynomial $y = 1.0 \cdot x^0 - 2.0 \cdot x^1 + 1.0 \cdot x^2$, yielding a 3-dimensional solution space.
- **Fat family:** A 10-coefficient polynomial with randomly drawn coefficients, yielding a 10-dimensional solution space.

**Experimental Setup:**

- For each $(w, h)$ combination, 5 random seeds were used; $N_c(w)$ is the minimum $h$ achieving zero violation in all 5 runs

**Tools:** NumPy for data generation and evaluation; PyTorch for network training; custom scripts for sweeping $w$ and $h$.
