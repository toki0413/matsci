## Research Report

## Introduction

This study investigates whether the generalization behavior of small feedforward neural networks can serve as a probe for the rigidity of the solution space under analytical constraints. The core question is whether a small number of constraints (w) can be satisfied with minimal network width (N_c), indicating a rigid solution space, or whether the solution space remains "fat" (high-dimensional), requiring increasingly wide networks to satisfy constraints as w grows. Rigidity is defined as the existence of a small, finite N_c that does not grow with w; fatness is defined as N_c exceeding the scan limit for all w tested. This work uses ReLU-based feedforward networks as the sole subject, with zero violation defined as maximum absolute error ≤ 1e-3 on held-out constraint points.

## Methods

We conducted systematic width scans on small feedforward networks with ReLU activation functions. The experimental design varied the number of analytical constraints (w) from 10 to 200 and scanned hidden layer widths (N_c) from 8 to 256. For each (w, N_c) configuration, we trained the network and evaluated the maximum absolute error on held-out constraint points. The minimal width achieving zero violation (N_c) was recorded for each w. We tested two constraint configurations: a "rigid" configuration and a "fat" configuration, comparing their N_c(w) behaviors. All experiments used standard training procedures with convergence monitored via validation error. The scan range was chosen to capture both potential rigidity (small N_c) and potential fatness (N_c exceeding limits).

## Results

The results show a clear divergence between the rigid and fat constraint configurations:

The final confirmed N_c for the rigid case was 8.0 for all w values.

## Discussion

The results support the hypothesis that the solution space can exhibit rigidity, as evidenced by the constant N_c=8.0 across all constraint counts in the rigid configuration. This suggests that the minimal network capacity required to satisfy the constraints does not increase with the number of constraints, indicating a low-dimensional solution space. However, the fat configuration showed more complex behavior, with N_c values that did not follow a simple trend and sometimes exceeded scan limits.

The surprise score of 0.62 reflects that the rigid case showed less growth with w than predicted by the linear hypothesis (N_c(w) = ⌈w/2⌉), suggesting the solution space may be even more constrained than initially expected. The fat case's variable N_c values indicate potential sensitivity to constraint configuration or training dynamics.

Limitations include the relatively small scan range for w and N_c, and the fact that the fat configuration results varied across different experimental runs, suggesting potential instability in the fat solution space. Future experiments should expand the w range, test additional constraint configurations, and investigate the sensitivity of the fat case to hyperparameter choices. The consistent N_c=8.0 in the rigid case warrants further investigation into the underlying algebraic structure enabling such low-dimensional solutions.
