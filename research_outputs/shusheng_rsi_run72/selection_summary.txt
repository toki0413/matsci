Three Divergent Hypotheses on Solution Space Rigidity

1. [COMPUTATION] — Linear scaling for floppy spaces: N_c(w) ∝ w
   Grounding: VC dimension theory (d_Vc(N) = Θ(N))
   Pro: Direct approximation-theoretic grounding; clear prediction
   Con: VC bounds are loose; may obscure small-w behavior

2. [GEOMETRY] — Polylogarithmic scaling for floppy spaces: N_c(w) ∼ (log w)^{1/c}
   Grounding: Manifold covering theory (N_cover ∼ (C/ε)^d)
   Pro: Connects to intrinsic dimension; distinguishes rigid from sparse
   Con: Hard to estimate intrinsic dimension from w samples

3. [OPTIMIZATION] — Square-root scaling for floppy spaces: N_c(w) ∼ √w
   Grounding: Hessian spectrum and effective degrees of freedom in loss landscape
   Pro: Distinct √w scaling; testable without computing Hessians; novel perspective
   Con: Near-zero threshold arbitrary; assumes specific constraint-degeneracy relationship

SELECTED: [OPTIMIZATION]
Reason: Most testable (N_c(w) measurement only) + novel (√w scaling from loss-landscape geometry, distinct from linear and polylogarithmic predictions)
