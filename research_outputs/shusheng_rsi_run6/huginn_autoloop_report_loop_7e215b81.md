## Introduction

The central scientific question addressed in this study is whether the generalization behavior of neural networks can serve as a probe for the rigidity of the solution space under a set of analytical constraints. In mathematical terms, we define *solution space rigidity* as the dimensionality of the function space satisfying all given constraints: a low-dimensional or unique solution space implies rigidity, while a high-dimensional or continuous family of solutions implies a "fat" (flexible) solution space. We investigate this by measuring $ N_c(w) $, the minimum hidden layer width (network capacity) required for a neural network to achieve zero violation on $ w $ out-of-sample constraints under a given constraint manifold. If $ N_c(w) $ remains constant or grows sublinearly with $ w $, this supports the existence of a rigid (low-dimensional) solution space. Conversely, if $ N_c(w) $ grows superlinearly, it suggests a fat (high-dimensional) solution space. This approach offers a purely computational, model-agnostic method to probe constraint geometry without invoking physical analogies or external domains.

In contrast, nonlinear constraints lead to superlinear growth in $ N_c(w) $, suggesting that the solution space, while still 1D geometrically, requires higher-capacity functions to represent due to the nonlinearity of the constraints. This implies that "rigidity" in the functional space (not just the geometric manifold) is influenced by constraint complexity.
