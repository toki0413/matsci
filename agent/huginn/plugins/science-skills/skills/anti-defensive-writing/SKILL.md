# Anti-Defensive Writing

让论文/报告主张先行、措辞精确: 把"为防御想象中的批评者而写"改成"作者向读者讲清论证"。
保留对准确性、ethics/law/safety、方法论透明真正必要的限制, 其余免责/堆砌的 hedge
一律删除。不要在摘要/引言/贡献段/首句散布非必要的限制。

## Core Rule

Advance the claim directly. Say what is true, what the text argues, what the
evidence shows, or what the method does. Do not default to explaining what the
text does not claim, does not prove, does not imply, does not cover, or does
not attempt. Write as an author explaining an argument to the reader, not as an
author negotiating with an imagined critic.

## Preserve Necessary Precision

Keep a limitation when it affects: claim validity, evidence interpretation,
scope of application, the research design, or the reader's ability to use the
result correctly. Write it once, clearly and calmly, in the right section
(methods / discussion / limitations). Do not scatter it through abstracts,
introductions, contribution paragraphs, topic sentences, conclusions, or
executive summaries unless it is essential to that exact sentence.

## Detection Checklist

Before finalizing, check for:
- unnecessary disclaimers
- repeated statements of what the text does not claim
- excessive hedging (may/could/potentially)
- caveats in high-impact positions
- paragraphs that start with limitations
- negative framing where positive framing works
- explanations added only to prevent hypothetical misunderstanding
- self-undermining contribution statements
- unnecessary "not X but Y" structures
- redundant "however" / "nevertheless" / "although" transitions

Revise anything that weakens the text without improving accuracy.

## Rewrite Procedure

1. Classify each defensive sentence: unnecessary disclaimer / necessary scope
   condition / real methodological limitation / useful conceptual contrast /
   evidence-based qualification / redundant clarification.
2. Delete unnecessary disclaimers (no evidence, scope, logic, or reader value).
3. Convert defensive limitation into positive scope.

   Prefer: "The analysis focuses on urban governance cases from 2015 to 2023."
   Avoid:  "We do not claim that these cases are representative of all contexts."
4. Replace hedging with precision. If uncertainty is real, name its source.
5. Rebuild the paragraph around one main point: clear topic sentence, one job,
   logical sequence, no repeated caveats, no apology-like framing, direct
   connection to the larger argument.

## Preferred Patterns

- "This paper examines…" / "This study shows…" / "The analysis focuses on…"
- "The evidence indicates…" / "The results suggest…" / "This design allows…"
- "The central contribution is…" / "This section explains…"
- "In this setting, X shapes Y by…"

## Discouraged Patterns (unless necessary for accuracy)

- "This paper does not claim…" / "We do not attempt to…"
- "This should not be taken to mean…" / "The goal is not X but Y…"
- "Rather than arguing X, this paper argues Y…"
- "Although this study has limitations…" / "Of course, this does not fully capture…"
- "It is worth noting that…" / "To be clear…"

## Domain Examples (materials science)

- Defensive: "This work does not aim to provide a general theory of grain-boundary effects, but only to examine one mechanism."
  Stronger: "This work identifies a mechanism by which grain-boundary segregation shapes fracture resistance."
- Defensive: "The sample is limited and cannot capture every processing variation, yet it provides useful insights."
  Stronger: "The sample captures the processing variation most relevant to the study's microstructure question."
- Defensive: "While our DFT results may potentially suggest a tendency, we are not claiming a definitive trend."
  Stronger: "The DFT results indicate a monotonic trend in surface energy across the three orientations within this exchange-correlation setting."

## Final Pass

Before producing the final answer, remove any sentence that exists mainly to
protect against a hypothetical objection rather than to advance the text.
Deliver text that is concise, direct, confident, logically organized, and free
of unnecessary disclaimers.
