## Research Report

## Introduction

The one-dimensional focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE),

\[
i\partial_t\psi+\tfrac12\partial_{xx}\psi+|\psi|^2\psi=0,
\]

admits the stationary bright-soliton solution \(\psi(x,t)=A\,\mathrm{sech}(A x)\,e^{iA^2t/2}\) (with \(A\) the amplitude). The existence of this solution rests on an exact balance between dispersion and self-focusing nonlinearity. A fundamental numerical-analysis question is whether a discretization preserves this balance over long integration windows, or whether a discrete Hamiltonian mismatch introduces a secular (linear-in-time) phase drift that eventually destroys the soliton. This report tests **Hypothesis B**: that the finite-difference (FD) discretization, unlike a split-step Fourier method (SSFM), suffers a discrete Hamiltonian mismatch producing a linear-in-time phase drift and eventual blow-up, whereas SSFM maintains the soliton with bounded error.

## Methods

Two integrators were implemented in `code_lab` (NumPy) on a periodic domain:

* **Domain and grid:** \(L=40\), \(n_x=512\) (so \(\Delta x=L/n_x\)), \(t_{\max}=10\), \(n_t=1000\) (\(\Delta t=0.01\)). The wavenumber array is \(k=2\pi\,\mathrm{fftfreq}(n_x,\Delta x)\).
* **Initial condition:** \(\psi_0(x)=A\,\mathrm{sech}(Bx)\) with \(A=B=1.0\) (so \(N_0=\int|\psi_0|^2dx=2.0\), \(E_0=-9.469977543812846\times10^{-4}\), \(P_0\approx-6.94\times10^{-17}\)).
* **SSFM:** Strang splitting — half-step linear propagation in Fourier space (\(\exp[-i k^2\Delta t/2]\)), full nonlinear step in real space (\(\exp[i|\psi|^2\Delta t]\)), then another half linear step.
* **FD:** A centered finite-difference discretization of \(\partial_{xx}\) with explicit treatment of the nonlinear term (exact scheme not recoverable from the truncated script; the observed instability is consistent with an unregularized explicit treatment).

Three refinement levels were scanned in [ev1] (trajectory L2, energy drift, momentum drift), a five-level SSFM refinement study was run in [ev2] to probe boundary-reflection/convergence plateaus, and the direct SSFM-vs-FD comparison was executed in [ev3]. ## Discussion

**Verdict on the hard criterion:** *Not met* for the stated objective under the FD protocol, and *unverified-as-passed* for SSFM. The objective requires \(L_2(t=10)<10^{-6}\), relative energy error \(<10^{-8}\), and relative momentum error \(<10^{-7}\). The best SSFM value available is \(L_2=1.6666596885140245\times10^{-8}\) ([ev3]), which is numerically below \(10^{-6}\), but the corresponding energy error is \(6.44\times10^{-3}\) ([ev3])—far above \(10^{-8}\)—and the momentum error is \(9.0\) ([ev3]), far above \(10^{-7}\). The [ev2] energy error reaches \(1.63\times10^{-9}\) at the finest level, but no matching \(L_2(t=10)\) and momentum pair from that same run is available in the ledger to confirm simultaneous satisfaction. Because these numbers come from different protocol families ([ev1], [ev2], [ev3]), they cannot be combined into a single pass verdict.

**Hypothesis B is strongly supported in its qualitative form.** The FD integrator diverges (\(L_\infty\sim10^{205}\), energy \(\sim10^{278}\)) while SSFM remains stable (\(L_\infty\sim9\times10^{-5}\)), which is exactly the signature of a discrete Hamiltonian mismatch: the FD scheme does not preserve the invariant structure that keeps the soliton bounded, and the error grows without bound rather than drifting linearly. The "linear-in-time phase" component is not directly confirmed as linear because only the fitted slope \(-9.42\times10^{-6}\) is reported ([ev3]); a multi-time-point slope regression is absent from the ledger.

**Surprise score 0.00** is appropriate: FD blow-up for an explicit discretization of a stiff Hamiltonian PDE is the expected failure mode, not a surprise. The genuinely noteworthy finding is the **momentum non-conservation** in SSFM (relative errors \(10^{1}\)–\(10^{4}\) across refinement levels, [ev2]), which is unexpected for a spectrally accurate method and suggests either (a) the discrete momentum observable is defined inconsistently with the periodic spectral derivative, or (b) the periodic boundary condition allows momentum leakage through the domain boundaries before the soliton tail decays to zero—consistent with the "boundary-reflection plateau" label in [ev2].

**Limitations.** (i) The FD script is truncated, so the exact unstable scheme cannot be audited. (ii) [ev1], [ev2], [ev3] use different parameter sets and must not be read as a single convergence ladder. (iii) The validation module flagged the raw output as unverifiable (score 0.3) because column labels, units, and a textual conclusion were missing—this is a real reproducibility gap. (iv) No multi-time-point phase regression was recorded to establish *linear* drift rigorously.
