## Research Report

## Introduction

The one-dimensional focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE),

\[
i\partial_t\psi+\tfrac12\partial_{xx}\psi+|\psi|^2\psi=0,
\]

admits a well-known bright-soliton exact solution. The central scientific question is whether a numerical discretization can preserve the analytic soliton's shape and its conserved quantities (mass, energy, momentum) to the stringent tolerances required for a *verification-grade* benchmark: \(L^2\) error below \(10^{-6}\) at \(t=10\), relative energy drift below \(10^{-8}\), and relative momentum drift below \(10^{-7}\). This matters because soliton-based benchmarks are routinely used to validate split-step and finite-difference codes before they are deployed on problems where no analytic solution exists. If the verification protocol itself is not numerically sound, downstream results inherit an unquantified systematic error.

## Methods

The intended protocol was to seed the simulation with the analytic \(\operatorname{sech}\)-profile soliton (velocity \(v=0.5\), centered at \(x_0=0\) on a domain of half-width \(x_{\max}=20\), i.e. \(L=40\)), propagate to check times \(t\in\{0,2,5,10\}\), and compare against the exact solution both in waveform (\(L^2\) norm) and in conserved quantities. Two numerical families appear in the evidence: a split-step Fourier method (SSFM) and a Crank–Nicolson (CN) finite-difference scheme. A symbolic residual check was also run to confirm that the candidate exact solution satisfies the PDE at the continuous level.

However, the execution record reveals a **broken pipeline**, not a clean numerical experiment. Two coder runs ([ev1], [ev2]) terminated at the maximum iteration limit with a `BashToolInput` validation error: the command argument was supplied as a JSON *string* where a JSON *list* was required. Consequently no filesystem or test-output evidence was gathered from those runs. The `code_lab` entry in Execution Data reports a symbolic residual of \(0.5625000000071144\) and identically-zero drifts, which is inconsistent with a functioning PDE solve and suggests a degenerate or unexecuted evaluation path. The only internally consistent numbers come from [ev3] (SSFM/CN comparison) and [ev4] (probe execution).

## Discussion

**Verdict on the hard criterion: not met.** The target \(L^2\) error of \(10^{-6}\) at \(t=10\) is exceeded by at least six orders of magnitude in every non-degenerate measurement (2.61 from [ev3], 2.062 from [ev4], \(2.2\times10^{24}\) for CN from [ev3]). The energy relative error target of \(10^{-8}\) is exceeded by five orders of magnitude in [ev4] (\(8.906\times10^{-3}\)) and by roughly seven orders in [ev3] (\(0.7005\)). The momentum target of \(10^{-7}\) is exceeded by roughly 31 orders of magnitude in [ev3].

**Interpretation.** The data do *not* demonstrate soliton instability; they demonstrate a **verification-failure mode**. Three independent pathologies are visible:

1. **Symbolic residual mismatch.** A value of \(0.5625\) for the exact-solution residual is inconsistent with a correct analytic ansatz under correct differentiation. This points to a bug in the symbolic check (e.g. incorrect phase convention or finite-difference stencil applied to a complex-valued function), not to physics.
2. **Catastrophic CN divergence.** The Crank–Nicolson branch reaching \(10^{24}\) is characteristic of an implicit solve with a nonlinear iteration that fails to converge, or of a boundary/aliasing instability, rather than of the physical NLSE.
3. **Inconsistent diagnostics across runs.** [ev3] and [ev4] disagree on the energy drift by nearly an order of magnitude (\(0.7005\) vs. \(8.9\times10^{-3}\)) and on momentum by many orders. Under the Provenance Rule, these must be treated as different experimental protocols — they cannot be averaged or reconciled.

The surprise score of 0.50 is appropriate: a well-resolved SSFM simulation of a single soliton *should* conserve mass to machine precision and keep \(L^2\) errors small, so the \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) waveform error is unexpected; but given the tooling crashes and degenerate zero diagnostics elsewhere, it is not *shocking*. The 0.50 reflects a result that is neither a clean confirmation nor a clean refutation — it is a corrupted measurement.

**Limitations.** No grid-refinement study is available (`convergence_orders` is empty), so the observed error cannot be attributed to spatial step size, time step, domain size, or aliasing. The two coder crashes ([ev1], [ev2]) mean that the intermediate validation and test stages never produced evidence. The `code_lab` zero-drift values (mass, energy, momentum all exactly 0.0 in Execution Data) are physically implausible for any non-trivial PDE solve and should be treated as unexecuted placeholders, not as results.

**Recommended next experiment.** Before any physics conclusion is drawn, the tooling layer must be repaired:
- Fix the `BashToolInput` schema violation (command must be a list, not a string) so that execute stages actually run.
- Re-run the symbolic residual check with a known-correct soliton ansatz (standard form \(\psi(x,t)=\operatorname{sech}(x-vt)\,e^{i[vx+(0.5-0.25v^2)t]}\)) and confirm the residual falls below \(10^{-12}\) before proceeding.
- Perform a convergence study: halve \(\Delta x\) and \(\Delta t\) independently, verify second-order (or spectral) convergence, and only then compare against the \(10^{-6}\) threshold.
- Replace the erratic momentum probe with the standard NLSE momentum functional \(P=\int \operatorname{Im}(\psi^*\partial_x\psi)\,dx\) and confirm it is computed on the same grid as the \(L^2\) error.

Until these steps are completed, the objective must be recorded as **undecided**: the evidence gap is not a matter of insufficient accuracy, but of an execution pipeline that failed to generate trustworthy numerical evidence.
