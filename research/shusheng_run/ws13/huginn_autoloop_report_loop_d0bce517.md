## Discussion

### Do the results support the hypothesis?

**Verdict: met — but only for the synthetic protocol, not for the real-data objective.**

Within [ev2], the directional hypothesis "group B mean exceeds group A mean" is supported: t(47.74) = 4.50, p = 6.69 × 10⁻⁶, mean difference 2.31 (95% CI [1.30, 3.31]), Cohen's *d* = 1.27. The effect is large and the interval is comfortably away from zero.

However, this finding is **tautological, not scientific**: the synthetic data were generated with a true mean separation of exactly 1.5 units (40.0 vs. 41.5), and the observed difference of 2.31 is a noisy upward draw from that known gap. The protocol *cannot* fail to support the hypothesis by construction — it is a power demonstration, not a test of an empirical claim. It therefore cannot be reported as evidence about actual penguins, actual bill lengths, or any real biological population.

[ev1] similarly demonstrates estimator recovery rather than discovery: the true slope was 1.5, the estimate was 1.36, and the CI covered truth. This is a methodological sanity check, not a novel result.

### Limitations (all genuine, all traceable)

1. **No real data was downloaded.** The single most important constraint of the task — a local CSV with verifiable provenance (URL, bytes, SHA-256, dimensions) — is unsatisfied. Every number in this report traces to a synthetic `code_lab` simulation. No claim about real penguin morphology can be made from this evidence.

2. **The confidence interval in [ev2] is a z-interval, not a t-interval.** The script used a fixed critical value of 1.96 despite computing Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (47.74). The correct two-tailed *t*-critical value at df ≈ 47.8 is approximately 2.011, which would widen the interval slightly to roughly [1.27, 3.35]. The qualitative conclusion (excludes zero) is unchanged, but the reported interval is anti-conservative and technically incorrect.

3. **The p-value uses a normal approximation.** The `erf`-based two-tailed p-value approximates the *t* distribution with the standard normal. At df ≈ 48 the discrepancy is small but non-zero; a proper *t* CDF evaluation should be used.

4. **No assumption checks were performed.** Normality, equal-variance (irrelevant for Welch but relevant for interpretation), outlier influence, and sensitivity to the random seed were never tested. With n = 25 per group and no real data, the robustness of the conclusion is unexamined.

5. **Single-seed results.** Both runs appear to be single-seed executions. No replication across seeds was performed, so the reported statistics are one random draw from the generator and carry Monte-Carlo noise that is not quantified.
