## Introduction

The one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE) admits exact bright-soliton solutions when dispersion and nonlinearity balance exactly. In computational practice, however, split-step Fourier methods introduce aliasing, roundoff-induced phase diffusion, and time-stepping error that can destroy this balance. The objective of this study was therefore not merely to reproduce a soliton, but to verify that a numerical solution remains within a strict tolerance of the analytic sech-profile: specifically, an L² error at t=10 below 1×10⁻⁶, relative energy drift below 1×10⁻⁸, and relative momentum drift below 1×10⁷. The energy drift (0.7013641921994364, [ev2]) exceeds the 1×10⁻⁸ threshold by seven orders of magnitude, and the momentum drift (9.625474×10⁶, [ev2]) marginally fails the 1×10⁷ criterion. The objective is therefore **not met**.

The near-unity variance slopes (~1.9998) across all resolutions are the central finding: they indicate that phase error grows diffusively with propagation time, consistent with accumulated roundoff or aliasing-induced spectral pollution rather than a fixed discretization bias. The fact that skewness and kurtosis remain nearly stationary further supports a Gaussian-diffusive error mechanism. The next experiment should decouple aliasing from roundoff by (i) fixing dt and varying the spatial grid to isolate spectral aliasing, and (ii) using extended-precision arithmetic to isolate roundoff, while reporting all errors in a single, labeled absolute unit system. A control run with an exactly conserved integrable quantity (e.g., the soliton norm) should be included to verify that the integrator itself is correct before attributing drift to physical mechanisms.
