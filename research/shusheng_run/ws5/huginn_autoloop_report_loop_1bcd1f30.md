## Introduction

The one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE),

$$i\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2} + |\psi|^2\psi = 0,$$

admits exact bright-soliton solutions when dispersion and self-phase modulation balance exactly. The hypothesis under test is the **sech²-mismatch hypothesis** (structure dimension): under appropriate initial conditions, a numerically propagated NLSE soliton should preserve its shape, with energy and momentum conserved, and the deviation from the exact solution growing no more than $10^{-6}$ over the integration window. This matters because soliton-based models underpin fiber-optic communications, Bose–Einstein condensate dynamics, and rogue-wave theory; if a standard spectral split-step integrator cannot resolve the dispersion–nonlinearity balance to the stated tolerance, downstream predictions (collision phase shifts, bound-state dynamics, perturbed soliton drift) inherit an uncontrolled systematic floor.

The objective's hard success criteria were: $L^2$ error at $t=10$ below $10^{-6}$, relative energy drift below $10^{-8}$, and relative momentum drift below $10^{-7}$, with convergence within ten iterations.

## Methods

$$\psi(t+\Delta t) \approx e^{i\frac{\Delta t}{2}|\psi|^2}\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left[e^{-i\frac{\Delta t}{2}k^2}\,\mathcal{F}\!\left[e^{i\frac{\Delta t}{2}|\psi|^2}\psi(t)\right]\right].$$

## Discussion

**Verdict on the hard criterion: NOT MET.** The $L^2(t=10)$ value of `2.038212e+00` from [ev1] exceeds the $10^{-6}$ threshold by approximately six orders of magnitude. The energy-drift criterion would be met *if* the reported `1.217293e-12` is taken at face value, but the primary accuracy criterion — waveform fidelity against the exact solution — is decisively failed. Momentum drift is not reported anywhere in the evidence ledger, so that sub-criterion is **unavailable under no execution evidence** and cannot be assessed.

**What the result most likely indicates.** The most probable cause is not a physical instability of the soliton — the exact sech soliton is orbitally stable under the 1D NLSE — but a **phase-convention or implementation mismatch** between the numerical scheme and the exact formula. Three specific candidates, in decreasing order of prior probability:

- **Sign/phase convention mismatch.** The exact solution printed in the script uses $\exp\big[i(vx + \tfrac{1}{2}(A^2-v^2)t)\big]$, while the standard Galilean-boosted NLSE soliton carries $\exp\big[i(vx - \tfrac{1}{2}v^2 t + \tfrac{1}{2}A^2 t)\big]$ — algebraically the same — but the *sign of the dispersive operator* in the SSFM (`exp(-0.5j*dt*k**2)`) must match the sign convention of the PDE. If the PDE is written $i\psi_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\psi_{xx} + |\psi|^2\psi = 0$, the Fourier propagator is $\exp(+i\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}k^2)$, not $\exp(-i\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}k^2)$. A sign error here produces exactly the observed symptom: excellent norm conservation (unitarity is untouched) together with a rapidly accumulating phase error that decorrelates the wavefunctions and yields an $O(1)$ $L^2$ discrepancy.
- **Velocity/boost implementation.** If the initial condition's phase factor or the exact reference's boost term is inconsistent with the numerical frame, the soliton will appear to drift relative to the reference, again producing an $O(1)$ error while preserving norm.
- **Insufficient resolution.** With $\Delta x \approx 0.039$ and $\Delta t = 5\times10^{-4}$, resolution is unlikely to be the dominant error for a unit-amplitude soliton, but it cannot be ruled out without a refinement study.

**Surprise score.** The recorded surprise score is `0.50` — neither predicted nor unexpected. This is appropriate: an $O(1)$ $L^2$ error is surprising for a well-known integrable case, but the validation subsystem's prior flagging of unverifiability makes the outcome partially anticipated. The score signals that the result should be treated as a **diagnostic failure of the verification protocol**, not as a physical finding.

**Limitations of the present evidence.**
- No convergence study exists. The "~2" order claim is an assertion in a print string, not a measured slope. Per the ledger, this is the central evidence gap.
- No momentum data were recorded, so the $10^{-7}$ momentum criterion is unevaluated.
- Only a single resolution was run, so the error cannot be decomposed into spatial, temporal, and round-off contributions.
- The single run cannot distinguish a phase-convention bug from genuine numerical instability; both produce large $L^2$ error but have opposite implications.

**Recommended next experiment.** Before any further physics claims, run a **three-level refinement sweep** in both $\Delta t$ and $\Delta x$ (e.g. $(N_t,N_x) \in \{(10^4,1024),(2\times10^4,2048),(4\times10^4,4096)\}$), extract the observed order $p$ from the $L^2$ errors, and simultaneously verify the sign convention by testing the *stationary* ($v=0$) case against $\psi(x,t)=A\,\mathrm{sech}(Ax)\,e^{iA^2t/2}$. If the $v=0$ case converges at the expected order and the $v\neq0$ case does not, the defect is isolated to the boost/phase convention. Only after the observed order matches the theoretical order of the Strang-split SSFM should the $10^{-6}$ tolerance be re-tested. Until then, the present `2.038212e+00` value stands as a **failed verification**, not a measured physical error.
