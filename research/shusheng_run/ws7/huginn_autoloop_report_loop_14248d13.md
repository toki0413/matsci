## Introduction

The one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE) governs wave propagation in dispersive nonlinear media, from optical fibers to Bose–Einstein condensates. Its fundamental bright soliton arises from an exact balance between group-velocity dispersion and self-phase modulation, and is analytically expressible in hyperbolic-secant form. A central practical question is whether a standard finite-difference time-domain integration preserves this balance over long times: specifically, whether the numerical solution remains shape-invariant and whether the associated Noether invariants (energy and momentum) are conserved to within the tolerances required for quantitative prediction.
