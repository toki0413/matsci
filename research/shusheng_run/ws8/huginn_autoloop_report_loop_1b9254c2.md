## Introduction

The one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE) governs the propagation of envelope solitons in dispersive, weakly nonlinear media. Its fundamental soliton solution arises from an exact balance between group-velocity dispersion and self-phase modulation. Under the standard normalization, the bright soliton is

\[
\psi_{\text{exact}}(x,t)=\eta\,\mathrm{sech}\bigl[\eta(x-vt)\bigr]\,
\exp\!\Bigl[i\Bigl(\tfrac{v}{2}x+\bigl(\eta^{2}-\tfrac{v^{2}}{4}\bigr)t+\phi_{0}\Bigr)\Bigr],
\]

which is a stationary shape-preserving attractor of the integrable NLSE dynamics. The central scientific question addressed here is whether a numerical discretization preserves this invariance to machine-precision levels over a macroscopic propagation window (\(t=10\)), or whether numerical discretization, phase initialization, and finite-difference truncation conspire to produce a non-vanishing error floor. Formally, the objective is: \(L^{2}\) error at \(t=10\) below \(10^{-6}\), energy-conservation relative error below \(10^{-8}\), and momentum-conservation relative error below \(10^{-7}\).

## Discussion

**Interpretation.** The data support a narrow but well-defined claim: the L² difference between a phase-shifted exact soliton and the same soliton perturbed at amplitude \(\epsilon\) is, to within relative fluctuations of order \(10^{-6}\), equal to \(\epsilon\) itself. In [ev2], \(\epsilon\sim10^{-9}\) produces a floor of \(\sim10^{-9}\) with median ratio \(0.9999999994954563\). This linear scaling suggests that, at these amplitudes, the perturbation is purely additive and no nonlinear amplification or grid-induced mode coupling is detectable.

**Does this validate soliton stability?** Not in the dynamical sense required by the objective. The hypothesis under test (Hypothesis D: "measure — phase-shift-induced L2 floor") concerns the *measurement floor* associated with phase initialization, not the long-time Lyapunov stability of the soliton under numerical propagation. A flat L² floor across random phases is a statement about the representation of the initial condition, not about whether a finite-difference scheme preserves the soliton shape, energy, and momentum over \(t\in[0,10]\). The validation block's evidence gap note is correct on this point: there is no propagation, no scheme, and no conservation-law trajectory.

**Limitations.** (1) No actual time integration is present in the evidence; the core objective criterion (L² error at \(t=10\) after propagation) is untested. (2) Energy and momentum conservation diagnostics are entirely absent. (3) The two probes use different perturbation amplitudes and cannot be combined into a single convergence order estimate without additional data. (4) The `score: 0.9999936754433681` in [ev3] is an internal objective metric whose definition is not documented in the ledger; it should not be interpreted as a physical error ratio.
