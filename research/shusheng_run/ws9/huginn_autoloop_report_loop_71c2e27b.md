## Research Report

## Introduction

The one-dimensional focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE),

$$i u_t + u_{xx} + 2|u|^2 u = 0,$$

admits a well-known bright soliton solution of $\mathrm{sech}$-type, whose persistence arises from an exact balance between dispersion and nonlinearity. The central question of this study is whether a numerical integrator can preserve that balance over long times to within a stringent tolerance: specifically, whether the $L^2$ deviation between the numerical and analytic soliton at $t=10$ remains below $10^{-6}$, with relative energy drift below $10^{-8}$ and relative momentum drift below $10^{-7}$. This matters because structure-preserving properties (mass, energy, momentum) are often used as proxies for the qualitative correctness of a simulation; a method that destroys them cannot be trusted for stability claims. The hypothesis tested here (Hypothesis C) is that a **symmetry-adapted error metric**—one that weights deviations by the underlying conserved quantities—provides a more discriminative probe of solver fidelity than a raw $L^2$ waveform difference alone.

## Methods

The workflow followed an autonomous hypothesize–plan–execute–validate loop. Four numerical schemes were benchmarked against the analytic soliton initial condition:

1. **`symplectic_split`** — a symplectic split-step (Strang-type) Fourier method.
2. **`ss_first_order`** — a first-order split-step method.
3. **`fd_euler`** — a finite-difference Euler scheme.
4. **`fd_taylor`** — a finite-difference Taylor scheme.

The target tolerances were fixed a priori as $\mathrm{tol}_{L^2}=10^{-6}$, $\mathrm{tol}_{E}=10^{-8}$, and $\mathrm{tol}_{P}=10^{-7}$. The initial soliton data yielded a mass of $1.9999999917511924$, energy $-0.33333333735839515$, and momentum $-1.9349595353548466\times10^{14}$ (per the benchmark run in [ev5]). A separate Crank–Nicolson implementation was attempted ([ev1], [ev2]) but did not complete within the iteration limit; its only available output is a partial file listing and a cyclic-Thomas solver sanity check with an error of $8.881784197001252\times10^{-16}$ ([ev2]). Two earlier completed runs ([ev3], [ev4]) used related but distinct protocols and are reported separately below.

## Discussion

The hard criterion of the objective is **not met**. In the definitive four-method benchmark ([ev5]), every scheme failed: the smallest final $L^2$ error recorded was $2.049635626844802$ (`symplectic_split`), which exceeds $10^{-6}$ by six orders of magnitude, and the smallest relative energy drift was $365.7$ (`ss_first_order`), exceeding $10^{-8}$ by roughly ten orders of magnitude. The momentum criterion fails even more dramatically, with the symplectic scheme showing a relative drift of order $10^{16}$.

Several observations follow:

- **The symmetry-adapted metric is discriminative.** It cleanly separates `ss_first_order` from `symplectic_split` by many orders of magnitude (scores $3.66\times10^5$ versus $3.11\times10^{16}$), so the probe is not void. However, it discriminates between *failures*, not between success and failure—no scheme approached the tolerance.
- **The surprise score of 0.50 is warranted.** The failure of a symplectic split-step scheme on a problem where it is usually the method of choice is unexpected, but the magnitude of the momentum blow-up ($10^{16}$) suggests a practical implementation issue—most likely an incompatible time step, aliasing on the periodic grid, or a sign/convention error in the split operators—rather than a fundamental limitation of symplectic methods.
- **The finite-difference schemes (`fd_euler`, `fd_taylor`) produced NaN**, indicating numerical instability (almost certainly an unstable CFL regime for explicit differencing of the dispersive term).
- **The Crank–Nicolson attempt is an evidence gap.** Because it did not finish, we cannot assess whether an implicit, energy-conserving scheme would have succeeded. The low-level linear solver test passing at $8.88\times10^{-16}$ ([ev2]) is encouraging but irrelevant to the nonlinear stability question.

**Limitations:** The reported benchmarks are not directly comparable across [ev3], [ev4], and [ev5] due to differing protocols. The [ev3] result, where energy drift briefly approached the tolerance ($9.56\times10^{-9}$), cannot be combined with [ev5] to claim partial convergence.

**Next experiment:** Re-run `symplectic_split` and `ss_first_order` with a refined timestep ($\Delta t \le 10^{-4}$) and grid ($N \ge 1024$), using the known $\mathrm{sech}$ initial condition and a reference SSFM solution at $\Delta t = 10^{-4}$, $N=1024$ as the ground truth. Complete and validate the Crank–Nicolson implementation, since its implicit structure is the most plausible route to satisfying the $10^{-8}$ energy tolerance. Only then can the symmetry-adapted metric be used to rank genuinely convergent methods.
